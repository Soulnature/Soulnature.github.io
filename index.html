I study how genetic variation shapes the human body as seen in medical images, with a focus on brain ageing and neurological disease. I use AI to turn brain and heart scans into heritable traits, then map those traits across the genome in large biobanks.

I build tools to make this possible at scale, including TorchGWAS, which runs genome-wide association tests for thousands of image-derived phenotypes on a GPU. My PhD in Biomedical Engineering at Fudan University covered neuroimaging genomics and gene regulation in brain disorders. Before that, I trained in computer science.

From voxels to variants

Each project follows the same path: from images, to learned traits, to genetic associations, to biological mechanisms.

  1. Images

    Diffusion and structural brain MRI and cardiac MRI from large population biobanks.

  2. Representations

    Unsupervised and contrastive deep learning that turns each image into compact, heritable endophenotypes.

    White matter FA maps, Nat Commun 2026

  3. Associations

    GPU-accelerated GWAS that tests thousands of phenotypes, including in samples with related individuals.

    TorchGWAS, Bioinf Adv 2026

  4. Mechanisms

    Enhancer–promoter maps, cell type-specific Mendelian randomization and gene prioritization to explain the hits.

    Brain EPIs, Genome Med 2023
